Raw. Pure. Natural. Which Honey Label Words Actually Mean Something?

By the True Finds team · August 2026

Quick quiz: on a honey label, which of these words has an enforceable legal definition? "Raw." "Pure." "Natural."

Mostly none of them, in any way that would let a regulator act on the word alone. That matters because honey is also one of the most adulterated foods in the world; blending cheap syrups into imported honey is a documented, recurring problem that testing programs keep finding.

What actually tells you something

"True Source Certified" means the supply chain was independently audited, which is the strongest signal on the shelf. A single stated country of origin beats "blend of imported honeys," because accountability follows specificity. Local honey with a producer's name and address carries a different kind of accountability, the kind with a person attached.

And one thing that fools people the other way

Crystallization is normal and natural. Real honey crystallizes over time, some varieties within weeks. A jar that stays perfectly clear forever is not demonstrating purity. Warm a crystallized jar gently in water and it returns to liquid; nothing went wrong.
