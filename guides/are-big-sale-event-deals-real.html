Prime Day, Lightning Deals, Flash Sales: How Many Are Real?

By the True Finds team · August 2026

Prime Day. Lightning Deals. Flash sales. Deal of the Day. If everything is a deal, nothing is, and the arithmetic of big sale events leans heavily toward "nothing."

What actually happens during a sale event

Amazon prices change constantly, hundreds of times a day across the catalog, and reference prices are chosen by sellers. In the weeks before a major event, it is routine for listed prices to drift upward so that event-day "discounts" measure against a taller anchor. The genuinely good deals exist, real lows on real products, but they sit side by side with typical prices wearing badges, and the two look identical on the page.

The tell is always the same

You cannot distinguish them by looking harder at the listing, because the listing contains only the numbers the seller chose to show you. The only evidence that settles it is the product's own price history: what did this thing sell for in March? If the event price is at or near the year's true low, it is a real deal. If the event price is what it sold for in March, you are looking at theater.

Practical moves for the next event

Decide what you actually want before the event starts, so urgency has less room to work. Note the current price of anything you are considering a week ahead; that one data point defeats most of the tricks by itself. Ignore countdown clocks entirely. And remember that genuine lows repeat: if you miss one, the history says another comes around.
