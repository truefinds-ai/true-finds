Prime Day, Lightning Deals, Flash Sales: How Many Are Real?

Prime Day. Lightning Deals. Flash sales. Deal of the Day. If everything is a deal, nothing is. Here is how to find the ones that are.

What actually happens during a sale event

Online prices change often, and the "was" price is chosen by the seller. In the weeks before a major event, listed prices can drift upward, so event-day "discounts" measure against a taller anchor.

GENUINE DEALA real low on a real product. The event price is at or near the lowest it has sold for all year.
TYPICAL PRICEThe same badge, the same red tag, but the price is what it sold for in March.

The two look identical on the page.

2

The tell is always the same

You cannot tell them apart by looking harder at the listing, because the listing only shows the numbers the seller chose. The evidence that settles it is the product's own price history.

If the event price is at or near the year's low, it is a real deal. If it is what it sold for in March, it is a typical price in a costume.

3

Practical moves for the next event

  1. 1
    Decide before the event starts.Know what you actually want, so urgency has less room to work.
  2. 2
    Note today's price a week ahead.That one data point defeats most of the tricks by itself.
  3. 3
    Ignore countdown clocks.They are built to rush you. A real deal is still real after a minute of checking.
  4. 4
    Remember that real lows repeat.If you miss one, another usually comes around.
