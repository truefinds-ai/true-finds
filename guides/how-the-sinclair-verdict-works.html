How the Sinclair Verdict Works: Four Answers, No Guessing

By the True Finds team · July 31, 2026
First published July 31, 2026 on the True Finds social channels.

When Sinclair, our free AI price protector, looks at an Amazon product, it reads 12 months of that product's real price history and answers one question in plain language: is this price actually good? There are only four possible answers, and each one is deliberately simple.

GENUINE DEAL

The price really is low for this product, at or near its true low over the recorded history. Not "lower than the sticker," which any seller can arrange, but low against what the product has actually sold for. A real low. Good time to buy.

INFLATED

The discount is manufactured. The "was" price was raised so today's price could look like a bargain against it. This is the verdict that protects you from the most common trick in online retail, and it is invisible without price history, because the listing shows you only the two numbers the seller chose.

TYPICAL PRICE

Nothing special is happening. This is roughly what the product usually costs. That is not a warning; if you need it today, the normal price is a fair price. It is simply the truth, stated so you can decide with accurate expectations.

NOT ENOUGH DATA

The product is too new, or its history is too thin, to judge honestly. This verdict matters more than it looks. A tool that always has an answer is guessing some of the time, and you cannot tell which times. Our standard is the opposite: every verdict backed by data, or no verdict at all. When we do not know, we say so.

Why plain verdicts instead of charts

Price history charts have existed for years, and most shoppers never use them, because reading a chart at the moment of purchase is work. A verdict is the chart's conclusion with the work already done. The evidence stays available for anyone who wants to look deeper, but nobody has to interpret a graph to know whether the discount in front of them is real.
