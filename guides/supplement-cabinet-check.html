Open Your Medicine Cabinet. Count the Supplement Bottles.

By the True Finds team · July 27, 2026
First published July 27, 2026 on the True Finds social channels.

77 percent of Americans over 55 take supplements regularly. Between the vitamins, the fish oil, and the joint support, the average cabinet represents hundreds of dollars a year of trust in what the labels say. Here is the uncomfortable part: almost none of us can check those claims, and the systems that exist to check them are effectively invisible.

The word "natural" has no legal definition

On a supplement label, "natural" is decoration. So are "supports," "boosts," and most of the vocabulary the industry runs on, which is carefully written to imply benefits without making claims a regulator could act on. This is legal, common, and worth knowing every time you read a label.

The FDA publishes warnings almost nobody reads

The FDA sends warning letters to supplement makers for illegal disease claims, contamination, and mislabeling, and publishes recalls week after week. These are public records. They are also scattered, technical, and read by approximately no one buying supplements on a Tuesday. A product sitting in your cabinet right now could have been the subject of a warning letter last month, and nothing about the bottle would tell you.

What you can do today

Look for third-party testing seals (USP and NSF are the meaningful ones; a seal the manufacturer invented is not). Prefer single-ingredient products over proprietary blends, because a blend's label can hide how little of the effective ingredient is inside. And treat any supplement promising results for a named disease as a red flag; that claim is illegal on its face.
